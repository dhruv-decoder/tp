## Key question 1: What statutes and rules govern AI today, and what is each one's status?

### Takeaway
India still has no AI statute. Binding AI-relevant law is (a) the DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025, in staged commencement through 13 May 2027 with a Data Protection Board that exists on paper but has no members; (b) the IT Act 2000 and IT Rules 2021 as amended on 10 February 2026 (G.S.R. 120(E), in force 20 February 2026) to regulate "synthetically generated information"; and (c) general criminal law (BNS/BSA 2023) and the Copyright Act 1957. The Digital India Act remains unpublished; MeitY announced an AI-law consultation paper in October 2026 that had not surfaced by 19 November 2026.

## Key question 2: What exactly do the final synthetic-media rules require of intermediaries, and from when?

### Takeaway
From 20 February 2026, any intermediary that enables creation or modification of "synthetically generated information" must label it prominently and embed non-removable provenance metadata; significant social media intermediaries must collect user declarations and technically verify them; and takedown clocks are 3 hours for court/authorised-officer notices, 2 hours for non-consensual intimate or impersonating imagery, 36 hours for other Rule 3(1)(b) categories, with grievances closed in 7 days. The draft's 10 percent visible-label size was dropped for a qualitative "prominent" standard.

### Cited Findings
- Notified 10 Feb 2026, effective 20 Feb 2026; new definitions in Rule 2(1)(wa) (SGI) and 2(1)(ca) (audio, visual or audio-visual information); timelines cut from 36h to 3h (court/authority orders), 72h to 36h (Rule 3(1)(b)), 24h to 2h (Rule 3(2)(b) nudity/morphed/deepfake), 15 days to 7 days (grievances) — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/government-notifies-information-technology-amendment-rules-2026)
- SGI = audio, visual or audio-visual information "artificially or algorithmically created, generated, modified, or altered using a computer resource, in a manner that makes it appear real, authentic, or true"; excludes text-only content, routine editing, document creation, accessibility improvements — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/)
- Rule 3(3): prominent label; "embedded with permanent metadata or appropriate technical provenance mechanism including unique identifier"; platforms must prevent removal/suppression of labels or metadata; the 10% size rule from the draft "was dropped" in favour of "prominent" — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/government-notifies-information-technology-amendment-rules-2026); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/)
- Rule 4(1A): SSMIs must obtain user declarations on SGI status, "deploy appropriate technical measures, including automated tools" to verify (hardened from "endeavour to deploy"), and label confirmed SGI; accuracy testing of technical measures at least every three months — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/ai-generated-content-deepfakes-524064)
- 3-hour takedown applies to notices from a court or an authorised officer of Joint Secretary rank or above — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/ai-generated-content-deepfakes-524064)
- Committee record: amendments "introduce a dedicated SGI due diligence framework under Rule 3(3)"; "user declaration, technical verification and prominent labelling of SGI" for SSMIs; removal of SGI by automated tools "shall not amount to violation of section 79(2)(a) or 79(2)(b) conditions"; exceptions for good-faith editing, accessibility and educational content — [27th Report](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/pdfName.pdf)
- Draft of 22 Oct 2025 had proposed label covering at least 10% of visual surface / first 10% of audio; MeitY FAQ PDF exists at https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf (not opened) — [Legal500 (draft comparison)](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/technology/it-rules-2026-deepfake-regulation-three-hour-takedowns-and-ai-labelling-obligations)
- Government's ATR to Parliament (6 Aug 2026) still describes the labelling standard as "10% of visual content", apparently referencing the draft — [The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/parliamentary-review-tracks-action-on-fake-news-deepfakes-and-platform-accountability)
- Gujarat HC (Apr 2026) directed platforms to onboard the Sahyog portal to comply with takedown duties; Supreme Court stayed that PIL on 12 Sep 2026 on the Union's transfer plea — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-stays-pil-proceedings-before-gujarat-high-court-deepfake-content-artificial-intelligence-sahyog-portal-union-plea-to-transfer-549809); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/supreme-court-stays-gujarat-hc-case-on-ai-deepfakes-after-centre-seeks-transfer-of-matter-to-sc)
- Conflict: one tracker lists 6 Oct 2026 as the commencement of the deepfake-labelling duties — [indiaairulebook weekly](https://indiaairulebook.com/this-week); contradicted by the official 20 Feb 2026 date in the [27th Report](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/pdfName.pdf) and all legal commentary. Treated as a tracker error.

### Inferences
- The 10% figure now survives only in the ECI's advisories (Oct 2025, 2026 elections), not in the IT Rules themselves.
- Enforcement is through loss of s. 79 safe harbour, not fines; the Sahyog portal is the operational channel for 3-hour notices, which is why its legality (X Corp appeal; SC transfer) matters for the SGI regime.

### Gaps

- No data on MeitY enforcement actions (notices issued, safe-harbour revocations) since 20 Feb 2026.

## Key question 3: What rights or remedies exist today for a person harmed by a deepfake, by instrument?

### Takeaway
There is no deepfake-specific statute. A victim can (1) demand platform removal within 2 hours (intimate/impersonating imagery) or 36 hours (other unlawful content) under the IT Rules as amended in 2026, with a 7-day grievance window and appeal to a Grievance Appellate Committee; (2) file criminal complaints under IT Act ss. 66C/66D/66E/67/67A and BNS forgery, personation, defamation, voyeurism and obscenity provisions; (3) sue civilly for personality/publicity rights and privacy, where Delhi and Bombay High Courts have granted dozens of injunctions since 2022 but since 2026 are limiting "blanket" relief; and (4) in election contexts, invoke ECI's 3-hour takedown directions. DPDP remedies (Board complaints) are not yet operational.

### Cited Findings
- IT Rules Rule 3(2)(b): 2-hour removal of nudity, sexual acts, "electronic impersonation, including morphed images and deepfake sexual imagery" on complaint; grievance officer acknowledgement within 24 hours; resolution in 7 days — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/it-rules-2026/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/technology/it-rules-2026-deepfake-regulation-three-hour-takedowns-and-ai-labelling-obligations)
- Failure of SSMIs to deploy deepfake-detection measures or meet the 3-hour mandate "strips them of their immunity under Section 79" — [Legal Service India](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/Legal-Articles/ai-deepfakes-right-to-privacy-india/)
- Criminal law: BNS s. 336 (forgery of electronic records), s. 356 (defamation); IT Act ss. 66C, 66D, 66E, 67, 67A, 67B cited by MeitY to the Committee as "actively used to address AI-related harms like deepfake pornography, AI-generated phishing, and synthetic media fraud" — [27th Report](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/pdfName.pdf); [Lawful Legal](https://lawfullegal.in/ai-generated-deepfakes-and-criminal-liability-is-indias-legal-framework-adequate/)
- Civil personality rights: Anil Kapoor (Delhi HC, 20 Sep 2023) first order naming AI, deepfakes and face-morphing; Aishwarya Rai Bachchan CS(COMM) 956/2025 (9 Sep 2025, Justice Tejas Karia, takedown in 72 hours), Abhishek Bachchan CS(COMM) 960/2025 (10 Sep 2025), Karan Johar CS(COMM) 974/2025, Nagarjuna CS(COMM) 1023/2025 (25 Sep 2025), Sri Sri Ravi Shankar (26 Aug 2025); Asha Bhosle COMIP 30262/2025 (Bombay HC, 29 Sep 2025) against AI voice models — [IPRMENTLAW](https://iprmentlaw.com/2025/10/06/personality-rights-and-bollywood-when-identity-meets-the-law/); [MediaNama Sept 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-karan-johar-aishwarya-abhishek-personality-rights-delhi-hc/)

### Inferences
- Remedies are strongest for celebrities with resources to litigate dynamic John Doe injunctions; for ordinary victims the practical remedy is the Rule 3(2)(b) 2-hour complaint and police complaint under IT Act/BNS. The Rajya Sabha Home Affairs Committee (Aug 2025), the CIT Committee (Mar 2026) and two private members' bills all recognise this gap by calling for a dedicated offence.

### Gaps

- Final disposal of the Rohilla/Rajat Sharma PILs after the Feb 2026 rules is unknown.

## Key question 4: What is the live status of each pending bill and parliamentary report?

### Takeaway
No government bill on AI or deepfakes has been introduced in Parliament (2019-2026). Five private members' bills exist and none has been debated: Deepfake Prevention and Criminalisation Bill, 2023 and AI (Protection of Rights of Employees) Bill, 2023 (both Mausam Noor, RS, introduced 7 Feb 2025); AI (Ethics and Accountability) Bill, 2025 (Bharti Pardhi, LS, 5 Dec 2025); Regulation of Deepfake Bill, 2025 (Shrikant Shinde, LS, 5 Dec 2025); AI (Human Health and Medical Education) Regulation Bill, 2026 (RS, 6 Feb 2026). ## Key question 5: Copyright and AI - DPIIT committee, litigation, amendments

### Takeaway
The DPIIT committee's Working Paper Part 1 (8/9 Dec 2025) proposes a mandatory blanket licence with statutory remuneration and no opt-out for AI training ("One Nation One License One Payment"); comments closed 6 Feb 2026, Part 2 is unpublished and the committee reportedly has not met since June 2026. Reports of an advanced Copyright Act amendment (July 2026) are unconfirmed. ## Key question 7: ECI, CCI, BIS and TEC instruments
